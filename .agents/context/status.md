## Slope anticipation (adopted)

- Finding behind it: braking on the setpoint only shaped the P error; at setpoint crossing the command was still 25-70 % (feed-forward + integral grown during the rise); in massive rooms 65-80 % of the overshoot was power applied above the setpoint.
- Mechanism (`smartpi/anticipation.py`): the P and I errors read `T + slope * horizon` (derivative on measurement, series PID form), correction bounded to 1 C and faded in near the setpoint (`clamp(2 - e/c, 0, 1)`); stable phase only; same rule in HEAT and COOL. Without the fade-in the rise time grows 10-35 min: rejected.
- Horizon: in HEAT the median of the kept lags of the heating balance (available within hours in every room), else the longer of the active dead time and half the passive dead time; in COOL the dead times only (the balance is measured in heating). Tried and not retained: the HEAT dead time alone (too short), half the cooling dead time alone (uneven), the sum of the two (too slow), the learned response lag (published in under half of the runs), the rise after a cut divided by the slope (2-3 times too short). In massive rooms every measured delay is about 40 % short of the best-braking horizon.
- Owner decisions: one mechanism for the approach to the setpoint, so the late-braking trajectory is removed, in both modes (one regulation rule for heating and cooling); its two configuration options and the published `setpoint.filtered_setpoint` are removed with it.
- Evidence in HEAT (simulation, frozen grid, screening then confirmation on fresh seeds, fast and slow emitters, valves, window and restart cells, field-like cells, 0.01 C and 0.1 C sensors): confirmed. Safety checks without any violation; median reduction of the mean overshoot of slow emitters 31-41 %; fast emitters, valves, windows, restarts equal or better. Thin margin on one row: in light-mass rooms with 1200-3600 s emitters, deep setback, cold weather, the time more than 0.2 C under the setpoint grows (within the allowance). 0.1 C sensors work with the same code.
- Evidence in COOL (six groups, fast and slow emitters): safety checks pass everywhere; mean overshoot down 73 % (median) with slow emitters, rise time equal or shorter. One comfort row is degraded in one group: 3600 s emitter in a light-mass room, time more than 0.2 C beyond the setpoint 20-43 -> 65-68 min per day. Accepted by the owner (same trade-off as in HEAT for that kind of room).
- Known limits: a field-like floor-heating cell shows no measurable difference (no rise reaches the setpoint in the sense of the metric); in fast light-mass rooms the balance keeps no lag and the horizon comes from the dead times; up to about 0.1 C more overshoot in a cold massive room on some draws.
- The product code was shown bit-identical in HEAT to the bench form that was confirmed; the regulation reference fixture of the test suite carries the anticipation.
- Weakness seen in the field (a tester's room, steady state at the setpoint): the slope is a least-squares fit over max(600 s, horizon / 3), so one quick temperature change of size s inside the window is read as a slope and extrapolated over the horizon: the correction reaches about 1.5 x s x horizon / window, i.e. up to 4 to 4.5 times s when the horizon is 1600 s or more (2.3 times at 940 s, under 1 at 320 s). With a sensor that moves by 0.05 C or more at once and a horizon from half the cooling dead time, the command swings by tens of power points around the holding power for about ten minutes, in both directions, while the room stays within a few hundredths of the setpoint; the deadband does not stop it because the P error reads the anticipated temperature. The owner objected to the command step of remedy (2) at the deadband entry; remedy (3) of the next item is the one in the code. Two remedies were screened under the frozen grid (ten heating cells, 0.01 and 0.1 C sensors, two draws): (1) a dead zone of 1.5 sensor steps on the fitted rise over the slope window: REJECTED, it removes the braking of slow rises (the sensor step has a 0.05 C floor, so slopes under about 0.45 C/h give nothing) and the median mean overshoot of slow emitters doubles (0.28 C against 0.14 C), with safety failures; (2) no correction while the regulator's deadband state is active (the state left by the previous calculation): CANDIDATE, safety and comfort unchanged in every cell (median mean overshoot 0.13 C against 0.14 C), no added command agitation, share of spurious corrections lower. Replayed on the recorded field day, both remove the swings; (2) keeps the braking of the landing until the room enters the deadband. The sensor models of the bench do not reproduce the field swings (command agitation about one power point without the remedy), so the benefit rests on the replay and the cost on the bench. To follow in the field: no more command swings in a settled room; coarse 0.1 C sensors are rarely inside the deadband and keep most of their spurious corrections.
- Deadband entry: cutting the correction at the entry moves the anticipated temperature back to the measured one at once, so the command steps while the emitter still has its inertia; on the recorded field day the step is large. Remedy (3): inside the deadband keep the correction until it is first zero or changes sign, then zero while inside (no step on the replay, swings removed as well). Screened side by side under the frozen grid (same cells, fresh draws): (2) CANDIDATE again; (3) REJECTED by the rules on one run out of 22 (room with light wall mass and a one-hour emitter, long time under the setpoint), passing everywhere else with slightly less command agitation. The bench does not separate them on the entry step (one to two power points in every arm, the bench rooms having a low proportional gain). A supplement declared before its runs (that cell alone, three arms, eight fresh draws) found the failing regime in none of the 24 runs: by its rule the failure is not attributable to (3). The owner adopted (3) by waiver of that rejection; (2) is not kept. To follow in the field: no command step when a room arrives in the deadband, no more command swings once settled.
- Model-update weakness (own topic, not started): in the room with light wall mass and a one-hour emitter the learned `a` moves by 25-40 % within a day in every arm, the reference included; the feed-forward follows at once and the integral (Ki at its floor) cannot absorb it, so the command jumps by 10-35 power points around an arrival. Usual outcome: about 0.5 C of overshoot at every rise (29 runs out of 30); rare outcome: arrival under the setpoint for hours (the rejected run above); in cooling one tooling run went 0.66 C beyond the setpoint the same way. Untested idea: a model update should not move the command by itself.
- To follow in the field: the horizon and its source (`analysis.control.anticipation_horizon_s`, `anticipation_horizon_source`) and the applied correction (`analysis.control.anticipation_c`) on real slow emitters.
